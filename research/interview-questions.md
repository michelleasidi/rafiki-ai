# Student Interview Questions

## Purpose

These questions are designed to explore academic cognitive load among neurodivergent university students broadly (including but not limited to dyslexia, ADHD, autism, and dyscalculia), rather than assuming a single condition or a single type of struggle.

The goal is to surface **specific, condition-relevant detail** while keeping the interview pool broad enough for realistic recruitment. Responses should be tagged by which condition(s) the participant identifies with, so patterns can be analysed both across the group and within specific conditions later.

---

## Opening (broad — lets the participant self-identify their experience)

- Walk me through a recent time you felt overwhelmed by coursework — what did that actually look like?

## Middle (surfaces condition-specific detail without assuming it)

- When you're stuck on a task, what's usually the actual blocker — not knowing where to start, not understanding the material, losing track of time, or something else?
- Is there a tool, app, or method you already use to manage this? What does it get right, what does it miss?

## Closing (tests the core product hypothesis)

- If something could simplify a reading, break down an assignment, and remind you of deadlines — all in one place — would that combination actually help, or would you still need something else too?

---

## Why this last question matters

This directly tests whether **integration** (bundling text simplification + task breakdown + deadline tracking in one place) is the actual unmet need — versus students preferring best-in-class single-purpose tools for each task individually. This is the central competitive risk identified in the [competitor analysis](./competitor-analysis.md): tools like Goblin Tools, which each solve one piece of this well, but neither bundles all of it for the university context specifically.

## Research framing

**Core hypothesis being tested:** Reducing academic cognitive load for neurodivergent students generally — not a single-condition or single-symptom tool.

This framing should stay consistent across the README, this document, and the competitor analysis, so findings can be interpreted against a single, clear problem statement.
